Claw Clips questions, answered

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

Claw clips are a gentle, stylish alternative to hair elastics, but choosing the right size, material, and tooth pattern is key to avoiding slippage or breakage. This guide answers the most common questions about materials, sizing, grip, care, and holding power so you can pick a clip that actually stays put and protects your hair.

What materials are claw clips made from and which is best?

Most claw clips are either plastic or acetate. Plastic clips tend to be very affordable and lightweight, but they can feel cheaper and may snap more easily if dropped repeatedly. Acetate, made from plant-based cellulose, is more flexible, durable, and often has a richer, marbled finish. It also springs back into shape better than plastic. For everyday use, acetate clips hold up longer and are gentler on hair because they have smoother edges. However, plastic clips can work just fine for fine or thin hair where you don’t need extreme tension. The key is to avoid any clip with sharp seams or rough edges, regardless of material, as those can snag and cause breakage.

What size claw clip do I need for my hair type?

Claw clips come in sizes ranging from mini to jumbo, but the right size depends more on hair thickness and density than on length. For fine or thin hair, a small to medium clip (roughly two to three inches across) is usually enough to hold a twist or half‑up style. Medium‑thickness hair often does well with a medium to large clip (three to four inches). For thick, curly, or very dense hair, you’ll likely need a jumbo clip, four inches or wider, to grab enough hair without slipping. Many jumbo clips also have longer teeth (around one inch or more) to penetrate a thick section. Remember: a clip that is too small will struggle to close all the way and will pop open, while a clip that is too large can feel bulky and may not get a firm grip on smaller sections.

Do claw clip teeth shape and grip really matter?

Absolutely. The teeth on a claw clip can be straight, slightly curved, or even interlocking, and the spacing between them affects how well the clip holds. Widely spaced teeth are better for thick, coarse hair because they can grip more strands without crushing them. Closely spaced teeth work well for fine hair because they distribute tension over many small sections and reduce slippage. Some clips have small ridges or texture on the inside of the jaw, which adds friction and helps keep the clip from sliding out. A clip with very smooth, polished jaws may look nice but can be prone to slipping, especially on shiny or straight hair. If you have slippery hair, look for clips with visible texture or a matte finish on the interior.

How do I make a claw clip stay all day without slipping?

Slipping usually happens when the clip isn’t grabbing enough hair or the hair is too smooth for the jaw texture. Start with clean, dry hair, product buildup or heavy oils can reduce friction. You can also try a small section of dry shampoo or texturizing spray at the roots to add grip before clipping. Another trick: twist your hair into a bun or French roll first, then insert the clip at an angle that catches the twist and some scalp hair. Pushing the clip deeper so the hinge sits closer to your head also improves hold. If you still get slipping, consider a clip with a metal spring hinge, these tend to have stronger clamping force than all‑plastic hinges, though they can snag hair if the spring is exposed.

Can claw clips damage hair or cause breakage?

Claw clips are generally much gentler than tight elastics or metal jaw clips because they don’t create a sharp demarcation line or friction point. However, they can still cause damage if used incorrectly. Leaving a clip in for hours while sleeping or wearing it in the same spot every day can lead to creasing or minor breakage along the tooth line. To minimize risk, avoid pulling the clip too tight against your scalp, and always release it gently rather than yanking. Also, be mindful of clips with sharp edges or exposed springs, those can catch and tear individual strands. If you have fragile or chemically treated hair, look for clips with rounded teeth and a hinge that is fully encased in plastic or acetate.

How should I clean and care for my claw clips?

Hair products, oils, and general dust can build up on claw clips over time, making them sticky or less effective. The easiest way to clean them is to soak the clips in warm, soapy water for 10–15 minutes, then scrub gently with an old toothbrush, especially between the teeth. Rinse thoroughly and let them air dry completely. Avoid using harsh chemicals or acetone, as they can damage acetate or dull the finish. Also, store clips in a dry place away from direct sunlight to prevent the material from becoming brittle or discolored. With regular cleaning, a quality acetate clip can last for years without losing its grip or color.

Frequently asked questions

Are claw clips better for your hair than ponytail holders?

Yes, for most people. Claw clips distribute tension over a wider area and don’t create the repeated friction point that elastics do, so they cause less breakage and less hair creasing. However, for high‑impact workouts or very active days, a scrunchie or soft fabric elastic may hold better than a clip.

How do I choose a claw clip for very thick or curly hair?

Look for a jumbo clip with long, widely spaced teeth and a strong hinge, preferably a metal spring hinge. The clip should be at least four inches wide and have a jaw that opens wide enough to accommodate your hair section without forcing it. Many people with thick curly hair also swear by clips with a curved jaw that follows the shape of a bun.

Will a claw clip work on short hair or a bob?

Absolutely, but go with a mini or small clip (around two inches wide). These are perfect for grabbing a half‑up section or holding back a few front strands. For very short hair, look for a clip with short teeth (about half an inch) so it doesn’t poke through the other side.

What is the difference between a metal‑spring clip and an all‑plastic hinge?

Metal‑spring clips generally provide stronger, more consistent clamping force and feel sturdier when opening and closing. All‑plastic hinges are lighter and can be more comfortable, but they may wear out or loosen over time. If you have heavy or thick hair, a metal spring is usually worth it for the extra hold.

Why does my claw clip keep popping open during the day?

This usually means the clip is too small for the section of hair you’re trying to hold, or the teeth aren’t catching enough hair to keep the jaw closed. Try using a larger clip or a different technique, like creating a tighter twist before clipping, to allow the clip to close more fully. Worn‑out hinges can also cause popping; if your clip is old, replacing it may solve the problem.

Can I sleep in a claw clip?

It’s not recommended for overnight wear because you might roll on it, creating pressure points or tangles. Loose, gentle claw clips used for a few hours are fine, but for sleeping, a silk bonnet or a very loose scrunchie is a safer bet for your hair health.

In shortThe best claw clip for you comes down to your hair’s thickness, texture, and the style you want to achieve. Focus on finding the right size, a smooth but grippy interior, and a hinge that feels secure. Treat your clips well and rotate them regularly to keep them, and your hair, in great shape. With a little trial and error, a good claw clip can become your most reliable styling tool.
